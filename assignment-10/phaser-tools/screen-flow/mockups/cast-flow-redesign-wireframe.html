Cast Flow, Reimagined

SpellTrialScene.ts's framed-panel pass just shipped and is correct chrome — the leather board, the filigree, the shipped pill buttons. This wireframe questions the shape underneath that chrome: a full-bleed opaque backing that hides everything behind it, one screen at a time. That shape fights the thing casting is supposed to feel like — reaching into what you're carrying and trying it on the world in front of you, not filling out a form that blacks the world out first.

Decided (Roc, 2026-08-23) — all four open calls from §6 ruled: satchel Option A (the strip itself becomes the picker); dock position is the bottom third, not a side rail; left-click stays examine and right-click fully replaces left-click as the cast trigger, no dual-path; VFX intensity stays uniform — no quieter version for a repeated trial miss. §6 below is now a decision record, not an open list.

Not final art — composition and interaction only, grounded in what's actually shipped: VfxSystem / VfxBackend.ts (cast VFX already exists and already knows how to anchor to a receiver's world position — CastScene.ts — or to screen-center when there's no receiver — CollectScene.ts), ReceiverHotspots.ts + HedgeCastPrompt.ts (today's left-click cast-on-a-thing), and SatchelStrip.ts (an always-on HUD row that already shows what you're carrying, today as text only). Right-click is unused anywhere in the codebase — a clean slot, not a collision.

§0

Why not hide the screen

A blocking modal turns casting into a dead end you have to re-enter: try, get told no, close, re-open, try again. An inline surface turns it into a loop you never leave — every failed guess is one click from the next guess, and the thing you're casting at stays in view the whole time you're deciding what to throw at it.

§4

VFX — always visible, because nothing's hiding it anymore

VfxSystem/VfxBackend.ts already exists and already knows two anchor modes — a real receiver world position (CastScene.ts) or screen-center when there's no receiver (CollectScene.ts, gated on mode.systems.includes("vfx")). SpellTrialScene.ts mounts neither today — a landed trial-cast is text only. The fix isn't new VFX, it's wiring the existing system into two places that don't call it yet, now that neither place blacks out the frame it would render into. Decided (Roc, 2026-08-23): one intensity everywhere — no quieter trial-miss variant. A miss doesn't play a burst at all (nothing landed); every landed cast, trial or world, gets the same weight.

§5

Accessibility pass

Two new interaction patterns this proposal adds — right-click, and a modal-less inline surface that used to be a dialog — each remove a default the browser/engine gave the old modal for free. Both need an explicit replacement, not an assumption that removing the blackout is purely a visual change.

Right-click has a keyboard path

A pointer-only gesture can never be the only way to reach a control (WCAG 2.1.1, Keyboard). Tab reaches a receiver marker, Enter/Space opens the same popover right-click does. Left-click's existing "examine" meaning is unaffected.

No focus trap, but a clear focus order

The old modal likely trapped focus by being the only thing in the frame. An inline dock/popover living beside real content needs focus sent to itself on open (the first chip or the try pill) and returned to the trigger on close — without ever preventing Tab from reaching the notebook page or the world behind it, since that's the whole point of not blocking it.

Cast outcomes are announced, not just shown

The old modal's result text sat inside a dialog a screen reader announces on open. A dock/popover that was already on screen doesn't get that for free — liveresult needs aria-live="polite" so a landed or failed cast is spoken, not just repainted.

VFX respects reduced motion

A screen-center or receiver-anchored burst is exactly the kind of motion prefers-reduced-motion exists for. Keep the outcome legible without it — the result text and the recipe's ✓/✗ marks already carry the information the burst is decorating, so a reduced-motion pass can drop the burst to a static ring or drop it entirely, not invent a second way to convey "it worked."

Icon slots meet the 44×44 touch minimum

The icon-strip picker (§3) reads fine as a dense HUD row on a 1920-wide desktop frame, but each islot needs a real 44×44px hit target regardless of its drawn size — pad the hit area past the visible icon rather than shrinking the icon to fit a cramped hit box.

Contrast holds on both new surfaces

The dock's scrim fades from transparent to opaque rather than a flat fill — check the --gold-on-scrim label contrast at the faded top edge, not just the opaque bottom, since that's where it's weakest. The popover's card background is already the same leather-modal fill the design system's contrast pass covers.
