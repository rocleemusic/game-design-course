# Vignette probe — Mara, the taking-down

**Status: probe, uncommitted.** Not a content deliverable. Written 2026-08-17 to answer one question from Roc: what does it look like if Mara opens up about Adren at length?

**Position:** end of arc. Final scene vignette — the class deferred to polish, probed here because the register question and the vignette question turn out to be the same question.

**Requires the loosened register.** Under the current one this fails outright — `L-V-7` breaks deflect-not-name, which is the whole point of showing it.

---

## The craft problem, and how it's solved

Her precision axis is **thing-versus-person**: she dates a hinge and prices a repair, and *"ask who the corner is set for and the sentences stop."* A vignette where she becomes fluent about her sister deletes the axis and she stops being Mara.

So she doesn't become fluent. **She talks about Adren entirely through objects** — hooks, creases, paper, dates — which is exactly what she has always done. The feeling arrives because the inventory is so exact, not because she explains it.

The long run is not the confession. The long run is **provenance**, which she is already licensed for. What the loosening buys is seven words near the end.

**The setting does the rest.** Her backstory is that the festival came anyway, the same week, the same lanterns, indifferent. So the vignette is the taking-down. Her tending finishing is the one thing her arc says cannot happen, and a taking-down is a tending that finishes.

## What's actually happening in here

**`L-V-2` — "Thirty-eight of them held something this week."** She counts. The three empty hooks are the entire grief and she never mentions them. This is `notice_and_want` verbatim — *the hook that held a lantern and now holds nothing* — and it is stated as inventory.

**`L-V-3` — the long run.** Sixty-two words about paper. Every clause is a fact: how you fold, why, who worked it out, what year, how many years since. The closing clause — *every one of these will open again* — is her belief stated as a property of the object. That is her whole cosmology and she thinks she is talking about creases.

**`A-V-3` — her hands stop.** This is the event. It is an action slot with no dialogue in it, which is the grief shape the corpus gives us and which survives the loosening intact, because it is technique and not affect. Her hands stopping is worth more than any line, and it costs seventeen words of description.

**`L-V-5` — "I keep them so she's not gone."** Seven words, and they are **Bex's sentence**, back in her mouth. He said *"You keep those so nobody's gone"* to her face in week one and nothing changed. Now she says it herself, unprompted, to someone else. The callback does the work — the line needs no weight of its own because the entire week preloaded it.

This is the one line the current register forbids and the loosening permits. Everything else here is legal today.

**`L-V-6` — "It doesn't work. I know that."** The trailing beat, one clause after the line looked finished. She is exact about things, and this is a thing: she has priced the repair and she knows it does not hold. Not self-pity. Accounting.

**`A-V-4` — the three hooks.** Named once, at the end, still empty. Never explained.

## What this costs, and what you have to rule on

**1. The loosening buys seven words.** `L-V-5` is the only line here that needs the register change. Everything else — the 62-word provenance run, the tense tell, the stopped hands, the empty hooks — is legal under the register as it stands today.

That is the finding, and it cuts both ways. The loosening is cheap and surgical. It is also less load-bearing than it looked, because the vignette's power is mostly technique you already have.

**2. This spends the axis, and it can only happen once.** Her engine is that the sentences stop when you ask about a person. Here they do not stop. That is survivable exactly once, at the end of everything. If she opens up like this every life, the withholding stops meaning anything and the arc is a mannerism.

So: **is this once across the whole game, or once per arc?** The reshuffle deals her a new role each life. If the vignette fires every week, `L-V-5` is worth nothing by the third one.

**3. Ending A or B.** A keeps the card as written and resolves nothing. B resolves the arc and cannot be taken back. My read is B, and only if the answer to (2) is *once, ever* — a finish that happens repeatedly is not a finish.

**4. Bex is not in this scene, deliberately.** He said the sentence first. If he is present when she says it back, it becomes a conversation between siblings and the player is furniture. Alone with the player, it is the player being handed the thing Bex could not get. His card's flag 4 — that his naming changes nothing — stays intact, because what changed it was the week, not him.
