The Festival Grounds of Hearthlight lie bathed in deepening dusk, the torches and lanterns still unlit save for the few dim ones clinging to the square's edges. The crowd is sparse but gathered with quiet purpose, a loose circle of townsfolk shifting slightly in their boots, their murmuring voices hushed as they wait for the ceremony to begin. At the square's center, a raised stone circle stands empty save for a single brass chime resting atop it, catching the last light of the day.

A figure steps into the circle — a priest, unremarkable except for the ritual distance that surrounds them. Their presence silences the murmurs of the gathered folk as they lift the small chime in both hands. A single clear note rings out, delicate and fleeting, yet it fills the space with an unmistakable command. The townspeople straighten, their chatter fading entirely.

The priest places the chime back on the stone and turns to a small wooden table holding a single candle. A small brass taper is already lit nearby, its flame steady in the still air. The priest takes the candle and brings the wick to the flame, letting it catch fully before setting it down once more.

They lift both hands, palms facing the flame. A word leaves their lips, soft and steady — "Waft." The air around the candle shudders almost imperceptibly, then the smoke rising from the wick stops drifting in the night breeze. It gathers, straightens, and rises in a single column, a clean, narrow stream that carries the sharp bite of pine and something deeper, earthier. The column does not waver as it climbs toward the darkening sky, carrying with it a fragrance that spreads outward over the gathered folk, crisp and bracing, like a sudden breath of air before dawn.

You inhale, feeling the air shift with the spell's presence. It is not unfamiliar — you've studied incense and wafting charms in your time — but never with this precision, this clarity. It stirs something in your chest, something restless and eager. Around you, others do not react as strongly, but their postures straighten nonetheless, as if the aroma has woken them from their usual evening drowsiness.

The priest turns, taking the still-burning candle, and approaches the first of the waiting lanterns at the edge of the stone ring. The wick meets the oil-soaked cloth inside, and the flame catches, spreading eagerly across the wick. The lantern brightens, its light warm and steady against the deepening twilight. The priest moves on to the next, and the next, each lantern taking the flame in turn, passing it on like a silent blessing.

You watch, the ritual unfolding before you in simple, quiet repetition. This is not the grand spectacle of a harvest festival or a solstice celebration — but there is something profound in its quiet steadiness. Each flame passed, each lantern lit, is not an explosion of power but a careful continuation, like a heartbeat that does not falter.

Then the last lantern is lit. The priest returns to the center of the ring and places the still-burning candle back upon the stone table. And then — without explanation, without any outward effort — the great Lantern Arch above the square ignites in a single breath of light. A dozen glowing orbs rise from it, hovering briefly in the dark before scattering upward, drifting like embers on the wind before disappearing entirely.

You stare upward as they vanish, the air still heavy with the faint echo of pine and earth. Whatever that was, it was not in the books you read, not in the charms you practiced. And yet, there is something about it that feels... right. Like the end of something small, the beginning of something greater.

The gathered folk do not cheer, do not murmur in awe. They simply watch, then turn away, nodding in quiet satisfaction before dispersing into the streets beyond. The festival is not done — there will be music, dancing, food — but this part, this quiet rite, is over. And yet, as you turn to follow the others, the thought remains. Another year in Hearthlight would not be wasted. That is not a certainty — just an idea, a possibility that settles in your mind like one of those drifting lights, there and gone before it can take shape. You let it go, stepping out of the square and into the night. The smell of the wafting smoke goes with you, sharp and clear, all the way back into the streets.
