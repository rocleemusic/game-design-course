# Cast (Souls)

The 8-soul roster, age bands, and the name/gender-fixed vs. role/age-redealt rule. See [`CONTEXT.md`](CONTEXT.md) for how this fits with the rest of the GDD. For the fill-in arc template used to write a soul's arc, see [`../narrative-pipeline/templates/arc-doc-template.md`](../narrative-pipeline/templates/arc-doc-template.md); for a worked example end to end, see [`../narrative-pipeline/examples/worked-example-mara.md`](../narrative-pipeline/examples/worked-example-mara.md) and the two real generated cards, [Toby's](../cast/toby.md) and [Ilsa's](../cast/ilsa.md). **Canonical cards live in [`../cast/`](../cast/)** — the copies under `pipeline-runs/` are frozen run artifacts and no longer match.

Every soul is built on the essence/role split (the `narrative-pipeline` persona-card schema): the **essence** — a want plus a repeated behavior — is invariant across lives; the **role** is re-dealt each new life from the shared pool below. The personality never changes; the job does. The full village is **3 deep + 5 texture (~8 souls)**; each life instantiates most of them — fate deals 1–2 as "past" (away or already gone), so every life carries a felt absence. Names below are working placeholders.

## The role pool

Roles are one shared, named pool — souls and the player both draw from it, and the pool is explicitly designed to expand on both axes (more souls, more roles) as the build grows.

| Role | Goal | Workplace — where the role is found, and when |
|---|---|---|
| **Mage** *(the newcomer)* | *Personal, not civic:* collect magic from around the world | *Roams — no fixed workplace.* The only role without one, because its goal is personal |
| **Blacksmith** | Forges the new Lantern Arch centerpiece | The Workshop (T4) · afternoons ⚠ |
| **Baker** | Prepares the communal feast | Market Row (T2) · mornings |
| **Postman** | Delivers the festival letters | Town Square (T1) + The Commons / Well (T3) · mornings, afternoons |
| **Herbalist** | Brews the festival tonic — shared at the festival as a wish of good health for the year, from the season's last herbs gathered ahead of the frost | Market Row (T2) + Forager's Clearing (F1) · mornings, afternoons |
| **Priest** | Leads the rite that lights the Lantern Arch and calls the souls home | Town Square (T1) + The Old Shrine (T8) · evenings, night ⚠ |
| **Farmer** | Brings in the harvest that feeds the festival week, not just feast night | Market Row (T2) · afternoons ⚠ |

**A soul is placed at their role's workplace, in that role's time blocks** (ruled 2026-08-13 — Roc). This is the rule that says where anyone can be found: the workplace binds to the **role**, never to the soul, because roles are re-dealt each life. Ilsa is at the Workshop only while she holds Blacksmith — next life she may be the Baker and belong on Market Row.

**This slice's deal** (ratified 2026-08-07, canonical in [`../cast/`](../cast/)): Mara → **Herbalist**, Toby → **Baker**, Ilsa → **Blacksmith**. Read through the table above, that puts Mara on Market Row and the Forager's Clearing, Toby on Market Row, and Ilsa in the Workshop — which is exactly where each one's scenes are authored. The texture souls' deal was completed 2026-08-20 and revised 2026-08-23 (see below): Juno → **Priest**, Pip → **Postman**, Bex → **Farmer**, with **Linnet and Nell dealt to past** — absent this life.

The values are authored in [`../tools/resolver/data/role-workplace.json`](../tools/resolver/data/role-workplace.json), each with a `rationale`; this table is the design statement they answer to. ⚠ marks a row the source flags `uncertain: true` — Blacksmith because T4's placeholder flavour is a general workshop rather than explicitly a smithy, Priest because T8 is the stretch 8th screen (fallback: T1 only), Farmer because no farm screen exists in the two-location slice and Market Row is the nearest fit.

**Why this is stated here.** It was not, until 2026-08-13 — the binding lived only in `role-workplace.json` and in the resolver that reads it, so the design intent could only be recovered by reading code. Stating it here makes it reviewable, and gives the resolver something to answer to rather than the other way round.

**The resolver already implements this rule**, in two places: `soulWeight` adds a `role_anchor` bonus when a soul's role works that screen in that block, and the guarantee floor holds each deep soul on a screen where they have an authored scene, rotating across a life so every scene screen is reached. Correcting an earlier note here: the resolver was never ignoring the binding. What misled the first reading was that `lantern-projects/v01`'s day files were generated **2026-08-01** and the placement logic was last fixed **2026-08-11** — the run folder predates its own fix, so measurements taken against it describe code that no longer exists. Regenerating the run is the action; no resolver change is called for.

Two things that remain true regardless: **the texture souls hold no role**, so the rule cannot place them at all (below); and **The Workshop (T4) carries a single afternoon NPC slot** while Ilsa's whole eleven-scene arc sits there, which rate-limits that arc to one scene per day whatever the scheduler does. Trail in [`../phaser/GAPS.md`](../phaser/GAPS.md) G15/G17.

**Texture souls' roles — dealt 2026-08-20 (Roc), revised 2026-08-23 (Roc).** The three open civic roles go to texture souls: **Juno → Priest, Pip → Postman, Bex → Farmer.** The 2026-08-20 deal had sent Nell to Farmer and dealt Bex to past; the 2026-08-23 ruling swaps the two — Bex moves into the Farmer seat, and **Linnet** (already dealt out, 2026-08-09) and **Nell** are now this life's two *past* souls — the felt absence. Six civic seats still fill six present souls exactly (Mara, Toby, Ilsa, Juno, Pip, Bex), matching the present-soul cap below.

The deal places each at their role's workplace in that role's blocks: Juno at Town Square + the Old Shrine (evenings, night), Pip on Town Square + the Commons/Well (mornings, afternoons), Bex on Market Row (afternoons). It binds **placement and identity only** — the texture souls stay social-only, with no authored workplace arc, so a role here does not imply scenes the slice has not written. Roles re-deal each life like anyone else's, so this is one life's deal, not a fixed station.

*(Parked for later expansion: Lamplighter and Village Chief. Village Chief was briefly un-parked on 2026-08-09 to seat Linnet and **re-parked the same day** when she was dealt out — six souls now fit the six civic seats exactly. — see [`../resources/parking-lot.md`](../resources/parking-lot.md).)*

**Mage is not player-exclusive.** Whoever holds it is "the newcomer" — a personal goal (collect magic from around the world), not a civic one like every other role's. On a new game, the player is always dealt mage and serves as the onboarding story. At the turn from cycle 1 into cycle 2 the player is given the option to continue in the save slot or create a new one. In the slice the option to continue is greyed out (scoping story content). On creation of a new save the player can choose from the full pool of roles — before the engine deals that life's present souls their roles from what's left. That pick locks for the rest of the life; it is not re-picked at later turns of the year. If the player keeps mage, they stay the newcomer; if they pick something else, mage re-enters the pool for a soul to be dealt, and that soul becomes the life's newcomer instead. For the slice only mage and blacksmith are available.

**Present-soul cap.** 6 roles remain for that life's present souls once the player has picked. "Fate deals 1–2 as past" above fits this almost exactly — 2 past (6 present) is an exact match; 1 past (7 present) is a role short until the pool grows. Lean on 2 past as the practical norm for now.

**Reuses the festival-goal engine.** A civic role's goal becomes the player's own personal contribution to the festival tier when they hold it — see [`03-core-loop.md`](03-core-loop.md)'s "Festival outcome" section. Mage is the exception: its goal is personal, not civic, so a mage-holding player doesn't contribute a role-goal to the tier this way.

## Texture souls (5) — social-only, one salient signal each, no deep profile

They counter-voice the deep trio so the whole village argues *"what is belonging?"* from every corner.

| Soul | Belonging-stance | One salient signal |
|---------------------|------------------|--------------------|
| **Nell** — the Content Server *(m)* | Needed — and at peace with it (a counter-voice to the Giver) | Hums while working; never keeps score. |
| **Juno** — the Found-Family Keeper *(f)* | Belonging is who you *choose* (counter-voice to the Kinbound; the game's own thesis) | Her "family" is a patchwork of unrelated people who all found each other. Advocate for "found family". |
| **Linnet** — Half of a Pair *(f)* | The one bond, out of reach — soulmates split by timing (the pairing-mirror) | Keeps a small habit for someone now married to another — a saved seat, a route past their window. |
| **Pip** — the Wonder-Seeker *(m)* | Belonging is in shared wonder, out there to find | Drags people to see small marvels; always mid-discovery. |
| **Bex** — the Rule-Breaker *(m)* | Says "you belong" plainly — the authored exception | Names the feeling out loud where everyone else deflects. |

**`suit_tag` is retired (RULED 2026-08-08).** Cards used to carry a `suit_tag` — `giving`, `keeping`, `blood`, `chosen`, `naming`, `wonder` — placing thematic links across the roster. It duplicated the **Belonging-stance** column above, which is the authority, carries more detail, and names the counter-voice relationships. Where the two disagreed the suit was the wrong one: it filed Nell with Toby under `giving` while this table frames him as *a counter-voice to the Giver*, and filed Linnet with Mara under `keeping` although their stances differ in scope. The field also lost its last unique consumer when soul-identity deduction was dropped (2026-08-09). Three cards carried open items asking what the suit hand-set was; all three closed with the retirement. **This table is the record. Do not re-add the field.**

*Stances and salient signals are locked here. Names and genders are settled, and both deep-soul behavior clusters are closed (2026-07-25) — Toby and Ilsa were each generated and human-gated through the crew.*

**Threads belong to the life, not to the soul (RULED 2026-08-06).** A soul's *threads* — the open questions the story keeps feeding — are dealt with the role and do not survive the reshuffle. When Toby is dealt Postman next life he gets **different threads**, not the same ones re-staged. What travels is the essence and the identity: the card, the conviction, the recognition hook. What is re-authored is the thread set that this life's role makes possible, because the role is what manufactures the pressure the threads run on (a Baker's feast cannot be finished alone; a Postman's rounds are a different engine entirely).

The consequence for the files: the cast card stays reshuffle-invariant and the thread registry moves beside it as a per-life sibling, `cast/[soul]-[role]-threads.md` (`GP-92`). A registry sitting inside a card whose stated job is invariance will read as canon and quietly outlive the life it belongs to.

**Name and gender are fixed; role and age are re-dealt.** A soul returns each life under the same name and the same gender, so the player can recognize them — the reshuffle changes their *position* in the world, not their identity. Making name and gender re-deal too would leave behavior as the only handle, which is the Obra-Dinn-style recognition puzzle this game deliberately parked ([`../resources/parking-lot.md`](../resources/parking-lot.md)). The promise in [`01-concept.md`](01-concept.md) — *a friend may now be a brother* — needs a recognizable person on the other side of it.

**The cast is 4 men and 4 women.** Nell is male on purpose: the Giver is a man who cannot stop giving, which inverts the usual coding of caretaking, and his direct counter-voice being a woman serene in service would have quietly re-installed the trope the Giver exists to break. The contrast between them is *earning versus ease* — it was never gender.

## Age is a role field

Each soul carries an `age_band` alongside its `role_tag`, and it is **re-dealt every life exactly like the job** — a soul is a pattern, not a station, and not an age either. Nothing on the essence side may depend on it, so a "youthful" or "world-weary" trait is a defect. Age is what makes the reshuffle bite in a channel the roles alone don't reach: a soul who was `older` last life can be dealt `young` this one, so every deference relationship inverts while the essence holds. It is also load-bearing for ordinary dialogue — who defers to whom, who is addressed as "boy", who mentors. Without it the crew invents age terms unlicensed and no consistency check catches them.

**Three bands, all role-capable.** The range is deliberately bounded: **every band must be able to hold every role**, or the reshuffle breaks — a soul dealt "child" could not run the bakery, and the role-deal would have to route around them, which contradicts the whole premise. Village life does the rest of the work; older people here still work.

| `age_band` | Rough years | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| `young` | late teens – late 20s | Addressed as "boy"/"girl" by elders; still proving themselves |
| `middle` | 30s – 50s | The default; peer to most of the town |
| `older` | 60s+ | Deferred to; addresses the young familiarly |

Three bands drive every address term and deference relationship without adding a band that breaks role-dealing. **Life-one assignment:** `young` — Toby, Pip · `middle` — Mara, Nell, Linnet, Bex · `older` — Ilsa, Juno.
