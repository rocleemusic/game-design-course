# World Bible

The world's canon — the festival, the town, the cosmology, the culture, the mage. Facts that hold across every life and every arc. This reads first, before [`01-concept.md`](01-concept.md), because the concept, the cast, and the arcs all stand on it.

---

## How to use this doc

**It is steering material, not player-facing text.** Nothing here is spoken as written. It tells a writer *what is true*; [`../narrative-pipeline/register.md`](../narrative-pipeline/register.md) tells them *how it may be said*.

**It is not the cast or the arcs.** The souls, their essences, and their arcs live in [`07-cast.md`](07-cast.md) and the cards in [`../cast/`](../cast/). One arc's steering lives in its arc doc, e.g. [`../narrative-pipeline/arc-festival-slice.md`](../narrative-pipeline/arc-festival-slice.md). This is the stage they stand on.

**Two layers, marked throughout.**
- **[lore]** — facts the player *can* be told, through souls and field notes.
- **[truth]** — facts *no scene ever states*. The player assembles them across lives, or never does.

State this rule once: a **[truth]** is never spoken and no soul knows it. Sections below do not repeat the caveat.

**Reshuffle-invariance — the hard rule.** Everything here survives every reshuffle. It binds to the festival, the town, the season, and the cosmology — never to a role-soul pairing, which re-deals each life. Test: if a fact goes false when the baker becomes the postman, it is not world lore. It belongs on a card or a per-life thread registry.

**How it reaches the player.** Two channels: NPC conversation and the mage's field notes. **Conversation leads.** The register loosened under T15 — warmer, wordier, room for a soul to recall, explain, and tell — so an NPC may carry real exposition. That is the intended path for lore: souls telling the player about their town, not narration.

**Consumer map** — which content reads which part:

| Content shape (ruling) | Reads |
|---|---|
| Intro VN scene (T16) | The Festival of Souls · Hearthlight · The mage |
| Spell-intro story beats (T15) | The mage & folk magic · Small canon |

**Upstream of the codex.** This doc is authored *forward*. [`../narrative-pipeline/npc-codex.md`](../narrative-pipeline/npc-codex.md) is the continuity ledger *downstream* — where the two disagree, the bible wins. The arc docs steer against this doc and no longer restate world lore.

## The hidden truth *(the spine — [truth])*

Everything surfaceable points at one hidden fact. The player assembles it across lives, or never does. **Ruled 2026-08-23 (Roc).**

> **Remembering is the engine.** What you tend, returns. A soul re-forms each **life** at the depth it was tended.

The town's faith — *the Lantern Arch lights the way so loved ones can remember them* — is not a comfort. It is the mechanism. Bonds carry across lives because a tended bond is the groove a soul re-forms into: tend one deep and the soul returns to you deep, let it thin and it thins. A soul dealt "past" this life is one no one held onto hard enough to bring back. The annual festival is the town's yearly practice of the same remembering — tending forward, in the open, once a year.

**The guard: loss stays real.** If tending simply returned people, neglect would cost nothing and the world would go toothless. Two brakes, both already canon:
- Some souls are dealt "past" each life ([`07-cast.md`](07-cast.md)). The deal is not fully yours to buy back.
- Loss is permanent (the not-Regenerative seed, [`../narrative-pipeline/register.md`](../narrative-pipeline/register.md)). What re-forms is tended *forward*; nothing un-happens. A soul who returns is not the loss undone.

**The magic is wonder.** Remembering-magic has a felt form, and it is wonder. It shows once a year as the festival's magic display, brightest at the Grand tier (see Festival night). It is the "great magic" the mage's rumor promised — the mage came to witness it without knowing what it is. Wonder is not decoration in this world. It is the shape the central magic takes, and it ties the display, the Alive axis, and Pip the Wonder-Seeker into one thread.

**A writer may show the truth working. A writer may never explain it.** A scene can show a warmth that re-forms or a return that lands, and never name the rule. The player earns the inference or misses it.

## The surfaceable world *([lore])*

### The world in a breath

A small hand-painted village and the woods at its edge, in a world where the year turns on a single night. People here stay put. The world comes to them in seasons — the first frost, the winter ferry, the festival — and they meet it by tending: a stall kept, a seat kept, a fire kept, a name kept. Work is close and domestic. Wonder is real but quiet: a thing done well, a thing come back around. Nothing hunts you. The stakes are only what slips away if no one holds it. The world's own sense of being alive starts low and grows the longer you stay (the primal seed, [`../narrative-pipeline/register.md`](../narrative-pipeline/register.md)).

### The Festival of Souls

Once a year the town builds, all week, toward one night. Its faith is plain: **the Lantern Arch lights the way for souls to return, so their loved ones can remember them.** For that night the returned and the living share the square, and no one has to say who is which.

The Arch stands over the Square and **ages year over year** — each festival the blacksmith forges a new centerpiece, so the Arch is a record of every festival the town has held. On the roads, the festival is spoken of as an **occasion of great magic**: the one night the Arch's light does what no ordinary cast can. That reputation is true and surfaceable. What the magic *is* stays hidden (the truth above).

**The centerpiece is crowned with a magical stone** (ruled 2026-08-30 — Roc). Each year's forged crown carries one raw ore at its peak that ordinary metal cannot stand in for: it **drinks and stores firelight**, and when the festival lanterns are lit it **takes that light up all at once and refracts it** — throws it back scattered, where the rest of the crown stays dark. In a good year it is a sight that stays with a person. The stone is not forged or faked; it must be found. This life the blacksmith is Ilsa, and the ore is the `ore_sourced` key item from the cave (F7) — without it the crown finishes only as far as its shape, and its state on the night is left unstated. *(Craft-fact only; never explain the effect in feeling or meaning. Surfaced in `ENC-ilsa-3`.)*

**The Arch's origin is lost on purpose.** It is older than anyone who tends it, raised by the founders and re-crowned every year since. No one living knows who first lit it, or why the souls answer here. No scene explains it, because no one in the world can. The town keeps the practice, not the proof.

### Hearthlight

The town is **Hearthlight** — the hearth a newcomer is welcomed to, and the light of the Arch. Belonging and the festival in one name.

Its character in one fact: **it takes people in.** The winter ferry arrives each year with people who have nowhere else to go, and the town finds them work and a seat. A village whose whole faith is not losing people is also the one that keeps taking new ones. That is why the newcomer mage is unremarkable here — arriving strangers are what the place is for.

The shape of the place, all ratified in [`../narrative-pipeline/npc-codex.md`](../narrative-pipeline/npc-codex.md):

- **The Square** — the heart, under the Arch. Fills at a morning hour and for the festival. Market stalls at its edge, the braziers, where the newcomer arrives.
- **Market Row** — the trade street. Herb stalls, the baker's counter nearby, the feast and the tonic take shape here. Lantern-hanging begins on the Row.
- **The Well / the Commons** — fresh water and the town's crossroads. The landmark all directions are given from ("left at the well").
- **The Lane** — the delivery district of cottages behind the center. Its own order run; a lamp lit at the lane's end for people walking home.
- **The Workshop / forge and yard** — where the blacksmith works the centerpiece. Bellows, slack tub, the piece in progress.
- **The Tavern** — a corner of the town scene, open only in the evening.
- **The Old Shrine** — an older, quieter site at the town's edge, tied to the rite and the Arch's origin. *(Stretch 8th screen; canon-light.)*
- **The Forest** — the woods beyond. Foraging, and where folk magic opens the way deeper. Wild-as-unvisited, not wild-as-threat.

Its past is shallow and mostly unspoken. One dated marker: **the flood-year**, far enough back that a child's toy carried through it in a coat pocket and the dye never came back. A date, not a disaster. Hearthlight lives in its year, not its centuries.

### The mage and folk magic

**Magic here is folk craft, not arcana** — learned by watching a neighbor and confirmed by doing, never from a book. Every spell is someone's ordinary work: the baker's *portion* parts a mass into equal measures, the blacksmith's *temper* sets a piece as it cools, the postman's *scratch* reaches an itch a body can't. The magic is woven into the trades, and the town would barely call it magic.

Spells produce **physical outcomes only, and the outcome is the receiver's** (the full rule is [`04-magic-system.md`](04-magic-system.md)). A cast can heat a stone or clear a hedge and can never make anyone feel anything. That limit is load-bearing: **connection is never something you cast.**

The mage is the newcomer — the one role whose goal is personal, not civic: **collect magic from around the world.** They roam, arriving in towns to learn the local folk magic. **Why here, now:** the Festival of Souls is spoken of as an occasion of great magic, and a collector does not miss that. That is the whole surfaceable premise — no private backstory needed. What the mage does not know is the truth above: the great magic is remembering, and its form is wonder. The T16 intro dramatizes the arrival and keeps the rumor on the surface.

## The festival week *([lore])*

The festival falls at the turn toward winter, on the edge of the **first frost** — the week's clock. The lead-up is a week (five days in the slice, up to three in the full game), and every day bends toward the night. Its shape is legible to a newcomer: lantern-hanging begins on Market Row and spreads; the **arch-raising** is a public work-party with a sign-up sheet, usually short a pair of hands; the drum band starts playing into the evenings; the Tavern fills after dark; the Square fills at its morning hour and, on the night, completely.

**The six customs.** Every year the town does all six, whoever holds the jobs. Customs first, role mechanics second.

| Custom | What the town is doing | Held by (this life) |
|---|---|---|
| The communal feast | Feeding the whole square on the night, no one turned away | Baker |
| The health tonic | A warm drink shared round the festival — a wish of good health for the coming year | Herbalist |
| The festival letters | Carrying word to everyone the town means to gather | Postman |
| The harvest | Bringing in what feeds the festival week, not just the night | Farmer |
| The rite | Lighting the Arch and calling the souls home | Priest |
| The Arch centerpiece | Forging the new crown the Arch wears this year | Blacksmith |

Read together, the six are one act: **the town gathering its people and its dead into one place for one night.** The feast seats them, the tonic wishes them well, the letters call them, the rite lights the way, the centerpiece marks the year. That is the surfaceable face of the hidden truth — the whole week is Hearthlight tending what it does not want to lose.

**The tonic (revamped 2026-08-23).** The herbalist brews it from the season's last herbs and shares it round the festival. Its meaning is a **wish of good health for the coming year**: everyone drinks, everyone is wished well. The frost is why it is brewed *now* — the herbalist gathers before the cold takes the last herbs, and that race is the herbalist's festival pressure. The sharing is what it *means*. *(Downstream reconcile open — see the appendix.)*

## Small canon *([lore])*

The concrete texture the field notes and greetings draw on. Kept small on purpose.

- **Foods.** Bread is the staple and the baker's stall the center (rye, white, seed loaves; a dark loaf for those who won't eat white). The **feast** is communal, cooked from the harvest, meant to stretch to whoever stands in the square. The **health tonic** is a warm herbal drink passed round the festival (above).
- **Sounds.** Sounds are collectible in this world — they travel free, like knowledge — so the town's noise is content: the **drum band** in the evenings, festival **bells**, a **hummed tune** at a working bench, the well's bucket, the ferry's bell in winter. The field notes and the `echo` spell both live off this.
- **Sayings and idiom.** Directions are given from the **well** ("left at the well"). Parcels shelve "lane names to the left." The town's word for staying is **to bide** — an offer to stay is "bide a while," and to be taken in is "a seat at the hearth." Nobody says the festival "works"; they say the souls "come home."
- **Materials.** The world is made of plain, findable stuff — river stone, wool, beeswax, salt, grass, dirt, sticks, tree sap, feathers, spring water, ash. Worked goods (wool, beeswax, salt) come off Market Row; forageables from the forest and outdoor town. One material is rare and story-bearing: the **special ore** for the centerpiece, not sourced in town, which unlocks a forest screen to reach.
- **The tending-customs (the through-line).** Hearthlight's people express care as **placement and keeping**, not as words: a **seat kept** for someone absent, a **lamp lit** at the lane's end, a thing **mended with a visible patch** (the mend makes it last without erasing what happened to it), a **name stitched** in a collar, a **corner set for two.** These recur across the cast because they are the town's shared language — and they are the everyday, surfaceable form of the hidden truth. The town tends absence in objects and never says why. A field note may describe one plainly and never name what it means.

## Festival night

The payoff of the week, and the place the hidden truth runs closest to the surface. A writer may show the truth *working* here and may never explain it.

**What the night is, on the surface.** The week's work comes due. The rite lights the Arch, the priest calls the souls home, and for this one night the returned and the living share the square with no line drawn between them. The lanterns are up, the drum band plays, the feast is out, the tonic goes round, and the town stands in the light it built. The souls come home to a town that made itself ready for them.

**The tiers — how much the night can hold.** The festival is one night, dressed across three tiers by how much was tended this cycle (the town finishing its customs, the player deepening bonds). Each tier carries its own magic display — the same remembering-magic at a different depth. **Grand is the top.** The tier itself is still never shown as a number — the night reads through its own brightness, not a figure. **A goal-completion count is shown now (Roc, 2026-08-31), reversing the earlier "never a score" rule for that one number only** — see [`03-core-loop.md`](03-core-loop.md)'s Festival outcome section for the exact scope of the reversal.

- **Quiet.** A modest festival. Few souls turn out, the square is half-full, the light low and warm. **Display:** the Arch lights, and a thin scattering of small lights lifts over the square and fades — the least the remembering can show. The night still lands. The vignette still plays.
- **Warm.** The town turns out. The square fills, every lantern is lit, the feast and tonic go round. **Display:** the lights over the square gather and drift, thick enough that people stop and look up, shapes almost forming in them — remembering visible, not yet at flood.
- **Grand *(the top)*.** The fullest turnout, everyone the town can hold, the Arch at its brightest. **Display:** the lights become the returned themselves — the town's own dead, and past them a glimpse of the **souls of the world**, all of it in one tableau. Remembering at full flood, and what it is, felt, is **wonder**. This is the great magic the mage came to witness, and the game's "going big" moment ([`09-art-direction.md`](09-art-direction.md)). The swell is entirely visual and sonic; no line explains the tableau, because the picture carries it.

**The impermanence guard holds hardest here.** Even a Grand night does not undo loss. The souls dealt "past" this life stay past. The brightness is remembering tended *forward*, never the dead brought back to stay. A festival-night line must never read as *they got them back*. A soul may keep a seat for someone who does not come, on the brightest night of all.

### Steering the night lines *(feeds T15)*

Every soul with a night scene stands on the Festival Grounds, playable in any order, on the last night ([`03-core-loop.md`](03-core-loop.md), the final sequence). The steer:

- **A soul speaks from where their arc landed.** The night is not new plot — it is the week's weight come to rest. The Giver who learned to be claimed stands differently in the square than the one who did not. Attendance is bond-gated (the T9 festival ruling): a warmer bond means they are there, and warmer.
- **Give the scene room.** Festival-night lines may run long and carry real exposition — the loosened register (T15) applies here. A soul may reflect, recall, thank, or tell the player something about the town, the year, or the person whose seat is empty. The night is where the week's meaning gets spoken, not swallowed. This retires the old "tightest words / weight preloaded" rule for these scenes.
- **The swell can live in the picture *or* the words.** When the display soars, the words need not soar with it — the reconciliation rule (visual/sonic swell, flat text) is available. It is now a tool, not a mandate: a soul may also say the thing plainly and at length.
- **A past soul's absence is a scene too.** Where a soul was dealt "past," the beat is their kept seat, their unlit lamp, or another soul quietly holding their place. The absence is authored, not skipped.
- **No soul knows the truth — not even on the night.** They believe the souls came home. They are right, and they do not know why it works. The line carries the faith, never the mechanism.
