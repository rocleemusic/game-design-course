## Save-state: what the save file records

The game tracks save-state across two boundaries: **within a life** (festival to festival, as the calendar turns) and **across a new life** (starting a fresh save slot, which reshuffles). A player can delete a single save slot to wipe just that life — the meta-hub and other slots are untouched. A hard wipe of the save data from disk erases everything, including the meta-hub; nothing survives it (a distinction carried over from v4, dropped in the v5 consolidation, restored here since it holds for all data, not per-slot). There are 3 save slots — three parallel lives on separate timelines; the meta-hub collection is shared across all of them, while each life's in-game home starts empty. On a true new game — no save data yet anywhere on disk — the player is always dealt mage. Once any save exists, creating another save slot lets the player pick a role instead, from [`07-cast.md`](07-cast.md)'s pool — for the slice, a choice between Mage and Blacksmith; the full pool is the eventual full-game set.

| Data                                                          | Within a life (festival → festival)                                        | Across a new life (new slot)                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Spells**                                                    | Yes, in the notebook                                                       | Yes, in the notebook                                                                               |
| **Sounds** (audio-objects)                                    | Yes                                                                        | Yes                                                                                               |
| **Physical items** (components, made things, mementos, tools) | Kept in your home across festivals; the satchel carries between locations  | **No** — the new life's home starts empty                                                         |
| **Bond level** (per soul)                                     | Yes, grows                                                                 | Yes, carries across lives                                                                          |
| **Roles / relationships**                                     | Fixed                                                                      | Re-dealt                                                                                           |
| **In-game home** (this life's décor)                          | Yes, accrues across festivals                                              | **No** — a new life starts empty                                                                   |
| **Meta-hub collection** (items *held* · sounds *heard*)       | Grows as you discover                                                      | **Yes** — permanent; shared across lives; new finds unlock as display pieces, never for use in play |
| **World-aliveness** (hidden; the world's Alive belief-evidence) | Grows with days played                                                     | **Yes** — permanent; shared across lives, like the meta-hub                                        |

*Roles are chosen once per life — the player's alongside that life's present souls, dealt from what's left after the player's pick — and locked for the life. On a true new game (no save data yet on disk) the player is always dealt mage; once any save exists, creating another save slot lets the player choose instead. For the slice, that choice is Mage or Blacksmith only; the full pool ([`07-cast.md`](07-cast.md)) is the full-game set. The only way to get a different player role is creating another save slot, or clearing an existing one down to no-save-data.*

**Saving is automatic — RULED 2026-08-19.** There is no manual save action and no save menu. The game autosaves each time the day or place changes. On boot, a **resume gate** (the Save/Load screen) reads the slot and offers **Continue** — drop straight back into the running life — or **Start over**, which clears the slot and begins day 1. A mode with no save, or a first run before the first autosave fires, passes straight through to the day-1 location pick.

## The game clock

Time in the game follows this structure:

- **Day.** A day runs **morning → afternoon → evening**. You open in the morning with a move budget (about 3–5 screen-moves); the time then turns to afternoon, where you may move to a new location or stay for a few more moves (about 3). The day ends in the **evening**, at whatever screen you're on — when evening-only spots like the Tavern are open — then you return home and pick the next day's location.
- **Festival cycle.** The lead-up to a festival night — one week (currently 5 days, expandable) in the slice, up to three weeks in the full game — then festival night and its ending vignette.
- **The turn of the year.** After each festival the calendar advances toward next year's festival; time passes, neighbors remember, and the next cycle begins in the same life. Continuing a life through further turns of the year now ships for the slice too (T13, ruled 2026-08-23, built 2026-08-24) — the rollover screen at festival night offers Continue into the next year in the same save slot.
- **A life (a save slot).** Many festival cycles, continuous: `cycle → festival → turn of the year → cycle …`. Roles are chosen once, at creation, and stay fixed for as long as that life runs; home, bonds, and collection accrue across whatever festivals that life reaches.
- **A new life (a new timeline).** Starting a fresh save slot reshuffles / re-deals roles (essence and personality fixed); bonds carry across.

**Bond bands, and the default (RULED 2026-08-06).** Content may branch on a soul's bond through a `bond_band(soul) = low | mid | high` predicate — the same beat authored three ways, which is where a lot of scene variety comes from. **`low` is the default and covers the zero and uninitialised cases**, which makes a three-way band fork **exhaustive by construction**: no `else` branch is needed and none should be written. Stating it matters because a designer unsure whether a fourth state exists will avoid the device entirely, and a fork whose gates can all be false soft-locks the scene between its entry and its exit. Engine verification and a test pinning zero-bond → `low` is `GP-115`. The band thresholds themselves are tuning, not design, and live with the rest of the bond mechanism.

**One attentive life may reach HIGH (RULED 2026-08-17 — Roc).** Retires the 2026-07-30 ruling *"one attentive life earns mid; high needs a second"* — restored 2026-08-07, superseded now.

*Why.* Authored content roughly doubled twice since that ruling, and `high_min` was re-sized twice to keep it true (36 on 2026-07-31, 82 on 2026-08-07). Toby's walked ceiling is now 174 against a bar of 82. The old ruling meant **high-band authored content was unreachable in a single playthrough** — which is all a demo gets, and reshuffle came out of the Definition of Done the same day ([`12-technical-overview.md`](12-technical-overview.md)), so the second-life payoff is not on camera at the capstone.

*What it costs.* Recorded in `12-technical-overview.md`'s Reciprocity row: three bands, and with HIGH reachable in life 1, later lives have no band headroom. Restoring the *"dialogue visibly warms over repeated lives"* target needs a band above HIGH, or per-life variants that do not key on band.

*What stays true.* Thresholds remain **tuning, not design** — they live in `tools/resolver/data/tuning.json` and are re-sized against measured ceilings, not chosen. `low` remains the default and the three-way fork stays exhaustive by construction. **Open, and blocking the re-size:** Ilsa's ceiling is 114 against Toby's 174 (her `trait_coefficient` is 0.7 — a guarded soul's bond moves slower), and thresholds are global. A `high_min` sized for Toby may put HIGH out of her reach entirely. That is `GP-21`, parked 2026-08-10 on a *"leave it"* and now load-bearing.

## Essence vs. bond — two things tracked, never feeding each other

**The essence side is fact** — assertable, confirmable, and revisable: what you have learned and can prove about who a soul is. It is what any future recognition gate would check. **The bond side is emergent** — a single hidden count that accretes from how you treat a soul, never shown and never split into stored sub-scores. It is what warms a soul's dialogue across lives ([`03-core-loop.md`](03-core-loop.md)) and produces the oblique reciprocity described below. Essence is deduction; bond is relationship — neither feeds the other.

**Soul-identity deduction is no longer required (RULED 2026-08-09).** The player is not asked to work out that the blacksmith this life is the postman next life. Nothing in the design depends on that recognition being winnable, and the retired `suit_tag` was its last supporting mechanism ([`07-cast.md`](07-cast.md)). **Scope of the ruling, exactly:** it drops *cross-life soul-identity* recognition. It does **not** drop recognition gates generally — the essence-side gates specced in [`../narrative-pipeline/pipeline.md`](../narrative-pipeline/pipeline.md) step 7 and the echo `payoff_condition` are a different mechanic and stand unchanged. The broader reading, retiring recognition gates as a class, was not applied and remains open. This split is load-bearing for the narrative pipeline: [`../narrative-pipeline/guardrails.md`](../narrative-pipeline/guardrails.md) check 2 (Superposition) enforces it directly.

## The persistence engine (runtime)

The shipped game's memory is ordinary game code, not a model call. A persistence engine remembers your incarnations, tracks the bond level per soul (the single hidden count above, accreted from weighted interactions across four action-categories — trust · intimacy · recognition · respect — with per-soul card-trait coefficients; the full weighting mechanism is specced in [`../narrative-pipeline/pipeline.md`](../narrative-pipeline/pipeline.md) step 9), gates the calendar, re-deals roles and relationships at life boundaries, and surfaces soft in-world reminders.

**That same hidden count drives the oblique reciprocity** ([`03-core-loop.md`](03-core-loop.md)): as it rises, the runtime selects warmer dialogue variants for that soul — a bond-driven text *selection*, not a new system. Runtime LLM calls, cloud-token usage, and AI cost are all zero at play time; the dev-crew's only obligation toward this system is to author the schema the runtime reads (see [`11-ai-agents-and-pipeline.md`](11-ai-agents-and-pipeline.md)).

**Permutation rules (ratified 2026-07-29 — rationale in [`../resources/permutation-design_draft.md`](../resources/permutation-design_draft.md)).** What varies between days and lives, all resolved by seeded host code from authored pools — never generated, never topological:
- **The map never permutes.** Screens, gates, and connections are fixed across lives (the metroidbrainia law); permutation touches contents only. A bounded screen-pool pocket is reserved as a full-game expansion slot, not in the slice.
- **NPC availability** is a weighted day-start draw into each screen's declared slot capacities — role-anchored (workplace + time_block), home evening-weighted, authored constraints — with one guarantee floor: **a neighbor with a live arc thread shows up when your chosen area is one their role reaches** (ruled 2026-07-30). Three things follow. **They stand where you can get to them:** the floor draws from screens open today, so a guaranteed neighbor is never left behind a locked door. **The role table decides where they can be at all** — a baker anchored to Market Row does not turn up in the forest, so picking Forest on a day his thread is live means that thread waits. Choosing an area is the day's commitment. **Their home screens are the open ones** — the hub and the screens open by default; the Festival Grounds carries a story slot too, drawn more rarely. The floor guarantees presence, never an encounter; souls without a live thread may sit a day out.
- **Items** roll per marked slot from weighted buckets at day-start; picked slots stay empty until the next day-start; key items and echo-carriers never roll — they are placed.
- **Determinism:** the day's seed is fixed by save slot, life, and day, so reloading re-rolls nothing.

**World-aliveness (ratified 2026-07-29).** A second hidden cross-life count: the world's own Alive axis (`../narrative-pipeline/register.md`, the player's primal seed — Alive starts low and moves). It grows from **days played, never from which options the player picks** — so it cannot become a niceness meter, the same guard as the bond. As it rises, the world quietly returns the evidence: the Festival of Souls display reaches its rarer dressings sooner, and rare authored manifestations enter the permutation pools (a spirit glimpsed in the forest). It is a *selection weight* on authored content, not a new system — the permutation model that reads it is `../resources/permutation-design_draft.md`. Like the bond: no UI, never printed, never gates an essence fact.
